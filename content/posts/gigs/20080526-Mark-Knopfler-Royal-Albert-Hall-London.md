The maestro delivers an intimate masterclass in a world class iconic venue.

<!-- excerpt -->

My first live concert was Dire Straits. I was 11, at Athletic Park in Wellington in 1985, on the Brothers in Arms tour, and my Dad took me. It was the first time I ever felt a kick drum in my chest, and I remember it like yesterday. I was raised on their albums, and Dire Straits are the soundtrack to my childhood.

I saw them again a few years later, at the same ground. That time we sat on the hill outside with my friends from school and watched the show over the fence for free. I didn't like the single so much, but it was still a good show.

So the opportunity to see the lead player, Mark Knopfler, at the iconic Royal Albert Hall in London on a Monday night, following a great weekend, was too good to ignore. My third time with Mr Knopfler, once in each of three decades, and he's better than ever.

This was a 6 night residency - testament to his stature in the music world - and he delivered a classy set of solo work mixed with Dire Straits classics. A more considered, more soulful Mark Knopfler now leads a band unafraid to experiment with traditional instruments and arrangements. Dire Straits never particularly concerned themselves with the rules of pop song composition; solo Mark has a complete disregard for them, and they play the tunes they wanna play, how they wanna play them. Surrounded by the best in the business, it's a formula few would dare attempt, and it works.

It ran for two and a bit hours, with three encores. The stand out solo tune remains **Sailing to Philadelphia**, plenty of numbers from the new album went down well, and to our delight there was room for a few Dire Straits numbers too. A couple of the other Dire Straits players joined him on stage for a song or two, as did renowned British musician Joe Brown (who I have to admit I'd never heard of). I have never seen a standing ovation at the RAH; this night we saw three. It was just like old times. Pure magic. The boy can still play.
